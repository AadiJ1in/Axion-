How It Works

A prescribed care plan, transformed into a motivating recovery journey.

Step 1

The therapist defines the clinical path

A licensed physical therapist selects the exercises, dosage, movement targets, and safety rules. Axion never replaces clinical judgment—it turns the prescribed plan into an interactive experience.

Step 2

Axion turns each session into a mission

Computer vision tracks reps, range, tempo, and form while patients earn XP, protect streaks, unlock milestones, and receive feedback calibrated to their recovery stage.

Step 3

Progress becomes visible and actionable

Therapists see adherence, movement quality, difficulty, and engagement patterns—helping them adjust the program before frustration, boredom, or poor form derails recovery.

Technology

Clinical-grade intelligence underneath a game patients can believe in.

The playful experience sits on top of a serious clinical system: movement analysis, therapist-defined guardrails, adaptive difficulty, and secure health data handling.

Perception

Computer vision

Tracking built on a standard camera identifies joint position and body pose in real time, processed on the device itself where possible.

Analysis

Motion analysis

Research

Built on a growing body of published research.

Axion's approach draws on an active, independent research area studying computer vision and AI for physical rehabilitation. These studies are not evaluations of Axion itself. They are cited here to show the evidence base behind the underlying approach.

JMIR mHealth and uHealth, 2025

Randomized controlled trial of a computer vision exercise application for knee osteoarthritis

Patients using a computer vision based exercise application showed improved physical function and self efficacy compared with a control group receiving standard care.

View study

Scientific Reports, 2025

A real time action scoring system for movement analysis and feedback in physical therapy

Researchers describe a human pose estimation system that gives real time feedback on exercise movement, and note its potential to support patient adherence to prescribed rehabilitation exercises.

View study

PMC narrative review, 2025

AI driven virtual assistants in home rehabilitation

A review of thirty one studies found that AI driven virtual platforms combining cameras or wearable sensors with real time feedback were associated with improved exercise adherence and fewer in person visits.

About Axion

Making rehabilitation clinically measurable—and emotionally easier to continue.

Axion began after extensive time inside physical therapy clinics, orthopedic research settings, and rehabilitation programs, working alongside physical therapists, orthopedic surgeons, rehabilitation researchers, and the patients recovering under their care.

Across every setting, the same pattern kept showing up. Patients received a carefully designed exercise program, then struggled to keep up with it once they left the clinic. This was not from a lack of effort. It came from a lack of structure, feedback, and accountability once no one was watching.

Axion was built to close that gap by combining clinically guided movement intelligence with thoughtful gamification. The goal is not to make recovery childish. It is to make progress visible, effort rewarding, and consistency easier to sustain.
